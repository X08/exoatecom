Textile Comparison // Hemp, Linen, and the rest

2 August 2026

A comparative look at fibers used in clothing, rated on tensile strength, durability, wash cycles, color stability, comfort, and ecological footprint (CO2e and water usage). LCA figures vary widely by farming method, region, and processing, so treat the CO2e/water columns as indicative ranges rather than fixed values.

Notes

  • Hemp and linen are the strongest all-round performers here — high strength, best durability, lowest resource input. Their main drawback is processing cost and a stiffer initial hand-feel.
  • "Bamboo fabric" is mostly marketing. Unless it's bamboo linen (mechanically processed, rare, expensive), it's viscose — the plant's low-input growing profile is irrelevant once it's chemically pulped.
  • Cotton's reputation as an eco-friendly fabric is questionable. Comfortable and durable, but conventional cotton's water footprint is genuinely bad; organic only fixes the pesticide side, not the water side.
  • Synthetics win on durability, lose on end-of-life — no biodegradation, ongoing microplastic shedding, and a footprint front-loaded into fossil fuel extraction rather than water use.
  • Wool's footprint numbers are contentious: livestock methane looks bad per kg of fiber, but a wool garment's multi-decade lifespan isn't reflected in per-kg LCA stats, which is a real methodological gap.
